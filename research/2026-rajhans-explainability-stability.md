# Beyond Gradient-Based Attacks: Adversarial Robustness and Explainability Stability in Cybersecurity Classifiers
- **Authors**: Mona Rajhans, Vishal Khawarey
- **Year / Venue**: 2026, arXiv preprint (submitted 2026-07-02, v1). Peer-review status: preprint-only as of 2026-07-14 (checked arXiv journal-ref/comments field — none present — DBLP had no indexed entry, Semantic Scholar lookup rate-limited/inconclusive). Note: the paper's own text states it extends the authors' *separate*, earlier conference paper "Empirical Analysis of Adversarial Robustness and Explainability Drift in Cybersecurity Classifiers" (arXiv:2602.06395), which the arXiv comments field confirms was accepted at ICAART 2026 — but that acceptance applies to the earlier MLP-only conference paper, not to this extended tree-ensemble/ESI paper (2607.01679) itself, which remains an unreviewed preprint.
- **DOI**: 10.48550/arXiv.2607.01679 (arXiv preprint DOI)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01679
- **Cluster**: 3
- **Dataset(s)**: Four tabular cybersecurity datasets — phishing URLs dataset, UNSW-NB15, NF-ToN-IoT, HIKARI-2021
- **Models / Methods**: Random Forest, XGBoost. Five adversarial attack methods tested, including black-box techniques applicable to non-differentiable tree models (ZOO, Square Attack) plus gradient-based ones (e.g., PGD)
- **Key metrics reported**: Apparent Robustness Index (RI) ≈0.98 for XGBoost under gradient-based ZOO attack (later shown to be a degenerate/misleading result); genuine RI ≈0.36 for XGBoost under black-box Square Attack; Explainability Stability Index (ESI, novel metric) 0.06–0.16 for XGBoost vs 0.14–0.29 for Random Forest
- **XAI method (if any)**: TreeSHAP, used to compute attribution drift under adversarial perturbation as the basis for the proposed Explainability Stability Index (ESI)
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Directly relevant to **RQ4** — introduces a concrete, reusable metric (ESI) for quantifying explanation-stability degradation under input perturbation, on a [0,1] scale parallel to a prediction-robustness index. Also relevant to **RQ3**, since it demonstrates that attack-method choice (gradient-based vs. black-box) can produce misleadingly high apparent robustness scores, i.e., a methodological artifact that could bias any explanation-robustness claim if perturbation design is not carefully chosen.
- **Stated limitations**: The authors report anomalous PGD behavior on z-score-normalized tabular data that required a separate step-size ablation study to explain; evaluation is restricted to tree-based models (RF, XGBoost) and TreeSHAP only — no deep learning, transformer, or Captum/Integrated-Gradients-based attribution is tested.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Under the black-box Square Attack, XGBoost's apparent robustness (RI≈0.98 measured with the gradient-based ZOO attack) collapses to RI≈0.36, while its explanation stability (ESI) degrades independently of — and sometimes more severely than — its prediction accuracy, indicating that adversarial robustness of predictions and adversarial robustness of explanations are distinct axes that must be measured separately rather than assumed to move together.
