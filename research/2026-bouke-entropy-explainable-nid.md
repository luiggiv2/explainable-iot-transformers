# Multi-Level Distributional Entropy for Explainable Network Intrusion Detection
- **Authors**: Mohamed Aly Bouke, Md Shohel Sayeed, Swee-Huay Heng, Azizol Abdullah, Mohamed Othman
- **Year / Venue**: 2026, arXiv preprint (submitted 2026-06-29, v1). Peer-review status: preprint-only as of 2026-07-14 (checked arXiv journal-ref/comments field — none present — DBLP, which lists only the CoRR/arXiv entry, and a general web search that surfaced no journal/conference publication).
- **DOI**: 10.48550/arXiv.2606.29797 (arXiv preprint DOI)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29797
- **Cluster**: 3
- **Dataset(s)**: NSL-KDD, CICIDS-2017, CICIDS-2018, UNSW-NB15
- **Models / Methods**: Training/dataset-free framework deriving interpretable multi-level entropy features directly from flow-level summary statistics (no raw packet data required)
- **Key metrics reported**: Weighted F1 ranging 0.708–0.989 across the four benchmarks; on CICIDS-2018, F1=0.74 but masked detection rate of only 0.48; on held-out (unseen) attack families, F1 exceeds 0.998 while detection rate collapses to zero; under temporal-shift testing, ranking quality is preserved (AUC=0.87) but detection rate at a fixed threshold collapses to 0.082; SHAP attribution stability: Spearman correlation 0.80–0.95 across cross-validation folds
- **XAI method (if any)**: SHAP, used both for feature attribution and explicitly for stability assessment across folds
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Highly relevant to **RQ3** — demonstrates that aggregate metrics (F1, AUC) can mask near-total detection failure on held-out attack families and under temporal distribution shift, which is precisely the kind of dataset-artifact/spurious-generalization risk our RQ3 aims to probe via XAI. Equally central to **RQ4** — reports SHAP attribution stability using Spearman rank correlation across folds (rho 0.80–0.95), the same statistic family we plan to use for our perturbation-robustness protocol, giving us a concrete comparison baseline for "typical" stability magnitudes in this literature.
- **Stated limitations**: The authors themselves flag that their entropy-only features, despite stable SHAP attributions, generalize poorly under temporal shift and to novel attack families — explicitly framed as a cautionary result rather than a shortcoming to be hidden.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Even though SHAP attributions for the proposed entropy features remain highly stable across cross-validation folds (Spearman rho 0.80–0.95), the underlying detector's real-world performance collapses under temporal distribution shift and on held-out attack families — showing that explanation stability alone does not guarantee generalization, a cautionary result for any XAI-IDS pipeline (including ours) that might otherwise treat stable attributions as a proxy for trustworthiness.
