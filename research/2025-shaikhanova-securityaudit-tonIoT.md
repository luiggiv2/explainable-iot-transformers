- **Relevance to our RQs**: Directly useful for RQ1 as a **comparable-dataset (TON_IoT) classical-baseline reference** with RF/XGBoost/LightGBM/Logistic-Regression per-class metrics reported to 4 decimal places — a template for how granular, reproducible baseline reporting should look in our own manuscript. The explicit finding that near-perfect aggregate accuracy (99%) masks a severe minority-class blind spot (MITM F1 = 78%) is highly relevant to RQ3 (systematic per-attack-category weaknesses / dataset artifacts vs. real signal) and motivates why we must report per-class F1 rather than accuracy alone.
- **Stated limitations**: High recall and low aggregate error rates conceal persistent weaknesses in minority-attack detection (MITM); high-cardinality text fields (SSL certificates, HTTP URIs, user-agent strings) were excluded to avoid leakage even though they likely carry MITM-distinguishing signal at higher layers; the audit does not capture multi-stage attacks evolving over extended time; DoS and DDoS are frequently confused due to near-identical flow-level signatures.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Across four classical/ensemble classifiers on TON_IoT, tree-based ensembles (RF, LightGBM, XGBoost) all converge near 99% accuracy and ROC-AUC ≈ 1.0 in both binary and multiclass settings, but this aggregate performance is deceptive — the paper's per-class breakdown exposes an MITM-detection blind spot (F1 ≈ 78%) that a pure accuracy/ROC-AUC reading would completely miss, reinforcing the need for mandatory per-class reporting in imbalanced IoT intrusion datasets.
