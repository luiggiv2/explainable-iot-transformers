- **Dataset(s)**: RT_IoT2022 (real-time IoT network traffic; 12 attack categories incl. DDoS Slowloris, SSH brute-force, Nmap scans, ARP poisoning, plus benign traffic from devices such as ThingSpeakLED, WiproBulb, MQTTTemp). NOT CICIoT2023.
- **Models / Methods**: Proposes "OTAD" (Optimized Transformer for Anomaly Detection) framework; fine-tunes and compares three encoder transformers — BERT-base-uncased, DistilBERT, and RoBERTa — after converting IoT traffic attribute records into natural-language text descriptions, tokenized to max 128 tokens via Hugging Face Datasets. Hyperparameters: lr 2e-5, batch size 128, up to 50 epochs, weight decay 0.01.
- **Key metrics reported**: BERT: training loss 0.0211 (epoch 34), validation loss 0.0677 (epoch 49), train time 22.80 min; DistilBERT: training loss 0.0154 (epoch 38), validation loss 0.1333, train time 11.42 min, inference speed 243.87 samples/sec; RoBERTa: training loss 0.0049, validation loss 0.1580, train time 21.08 min. (Loss-based reporting; accuracy/F1/ROC-AUC not confirmed from fetched content — `[VERIFICAR]`.)
- **XAI method (if any)**: None formally applied; authors mention self-attention "captures intricate dependencies" but do not perform attention visualization, SHAP, or Captum-based attribution.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: **Direct competitor for RQ1 and RQ5.** Nearly identical experimental skeleton to ours — textualize IoT traffic records, fine-tune DistilBERT (and BERT/RoBERTa) as classifiers, and explicitly compare training time / inference speed as a resource-constrained-deployment argument (their DistilBERT-vs-BERT comparison maps directly onto our RQ5 accuracy/latency/footprint trade-off). Key gap for us to fill: no explainability analysis at all, no classical-ML baselines (RF/SVM/XGBoost) for RQ1 comparison, and evaluated on RT_IoT2022 rather than CICIoT2023.
- **Stated limitations**: Performance sensitive to attack-category distribution/representativeness of training data (risk with rare/novel attacks); text-based feature conversion may lose fine-grained packet-level/temporal characteristics; computational cost still a concern for genuine real-time on-device deployment; single-dataset validation limits generalization claims; structured-text conversion process risks losing granular temporal/behavioral patterns.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: When resource-constrained IoT deployment is the objective, DistilBERT offers roughly half the training time and markedly faster inference than full BERT with only a moderate increase in validation loss, making it the more practical text-based transformer classifier for edge intrusion detection — even though the paper does not test whether this efficiency gain costs any classification accuracy/F1 or explainability.
