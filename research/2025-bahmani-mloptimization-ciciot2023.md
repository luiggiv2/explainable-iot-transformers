- **XAI method (if any)**: None identified — confirmed via full primary text (no SHAP/LIME/Captum/attention-analysis section anywhere in the XML); paper focus is hyperparameter optimization and accuracy/compute-time trade-offs, not explainability.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Offers a directly comparable **classical-baseline (RF vs. XGBoost) result on CICIoT2023** for RQ1, notable because default/basic RF (0.75 accuracy) underperforms XGBoost (0.85) while hyperparameter-optimized RF (0.91) surpasses it — a caution for our own experimental design that baseline comparisons must control for tuning effort or risk mischaracterizing which classical algorithm is "best." Also reports training/inference compute time per model, directly relevant to RQ5 (accuracy/latency/footprint trade-off), giving a classical-model reference point to compare against our transformer's inference cost. The per-class breakdown is now fully confirmed and independently reinforces our RQ1 framing that "never accuracy alone" is essential: basic RF and XGBoost both silently fail (0.00 P/R/F1) on 2–4 of 6 classes despite respectable overall accuracy — directly supporting the methodology note in our own project's hard rules on reporting per-class metrics.
- **Stated limitations**: No dedicated "Limitations" section exists in the paper (confirmed from primary source — the section list is Introduction, Related Works, Intrusion Detection System for IoT, Experimentation process, Dataset, Performance Metrics, Results and Discussion, Conclusion; no separate Limitations/Future Work heading). The Conclusion states verbatim: "In future work, further studies will focus on the most effective machine learning techniques for intrusion detection systems in IoT. Our goal is to develop an intrusion detection system that can accurately detect all types of attacks in the IoT network and ensure better performance." No dataset-specific limitations (e.g., class imbalance handling, generalization, train/test split methodology) are discussed beyond this generic future-work statement — this is a genuine limitation of the paper itself, not a gap in our verification.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Hyperparameter tuning changes the ranking of classical vs. boosted-tree baselines on CICIoT2023 — an untuned Random Forest (0.75 accuracy) actually underperforms untuned XGBoost (0.85), but a HalvingGridSearchCV-optimized Random Forest (0.91) outperforms both, while optimization gives negligible benefit to MLP — illustrating that "RF vs. XGBoost" comparisons on this dataset are highly sensitive to tuning budget and should be reported with tuning methodology, not treated as fixed algorithm rankings. Verbatim from the paper's Conclusion: "This method successfully detected all types of attacks, while most of the other methods failed to detect attacks in at least one class" — the optimized RF is the only classifier in the study with nonzero recall across all 6 attack classes.
