- **Dataset(s)**: ToN_IoT (190,474 samples, 10 attack categories, 42 features), Edge-IIoTset (~1.9M samples, 15 classes, 61 features, MQTT/Modbus protocols), X-IIoTID (814,612 samples, 19 classes, 66 features, incl. reconnaissance and insider-threat classes). NOT CICIoT2023.
- **Models / Methods**: Stacked ensemble of three lightweight BERT-family base learners — DistilBERT (~66M params), MobileBERT (~25M params), TinyBERT (~15M params) — each fine-tuned independently on tabular flow records converted to space-delimited text strings (e.g. "0.0.0.10 172.16.0.5 443 8080 UDP"), tokenized via AutoTokenizer; [CLS]-token embeddings/probability outputs from all three models concatenated into a feature vector fed to a Random Forest meta-classifier. SMOTE–Tomek resampling applied only to the training split (60/20/20 split) for class imbalance.
- **Key metrics reported**: Edge-IIoTset: Acc 97.11%, Prec 97.29%, Rec 97.11%, F1 97.13%; ToN_IoT: Acc/Prec/Rec/F1 all ≈99.46%; X-IIoTID: Acc/Prec/Rec/F1 = 100.00%. Per-class scores drop notably on rare classes (e.g., Fingerprinting ~78%, XSS ~85% on Edge-IIoTset).
- **XAI method (if any)**: No formal XAI framework (no SHAP/Captum/IG). Authors only note that the Random Forest meta-classifier offers "intrinsic interpretability through feature importance," without a detailed attribution analysis.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: **Direct competitor / closest prior art for RQ1.** Uses essentially the same core recipe we propose — textualize tabular IoT flow features into a sentence, fine-tune DistilBERT and TinyBERT as classifiers — but (a) evaluates on ToN_IoT/Edge-IIoTset/X-IIoTID rather than CICIoT2023, (b) uses an ensemble+meta-classifier architecture rather than a single interpretable encoder, and (c) has no Captum/SHAP-based explainability (RQ2–RQ4 gap). This is the strongest single "prior art" paper to differentiate against in the introduction/related work.
- **Stated limitations**: Domain-specific (IoT/IIoT only, needs re-tuning to transfer); trained on fixed/known attack snapshots so zero-day/novel variants may fall outside distribution; SMOTE–Tomek oversampling may artificially inflate minority-class metrics ("optimistic" results); textual conversion pipeline is dataset-specific and needs re-validation for new deployments.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Fine-tuning three lightweight BERT variants on space-delimited textualized IoT flow records and stacking their outputs through a Random Forest meta-classifier reaches near-perfect detection accuracy on IIoT benchmarks while remaining far cheaper than full-size BERT, but the paper stops short of any formal, token- or feature-level explainability analysis of the fine-tuned encoders themselves.
