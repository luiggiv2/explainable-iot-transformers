- **Relevance to our RQs**: Central to RQ3. Enumerates eight concrete, citable limitation categories of CICIoT2023 that are plausible sources of spurious model shortcuts: (1) severe class imbalance (benign/DDoS traffic dominate; brute-force and web-based classes are each well under 1% of records); (2) limited IoT device diversity (single simulated smart-home topology, not industrial/health IoT); (3) absence of encrypted traffic; (4) simplified, single-technique attack scenarios that do not capture real attackers combining/adapting methods; (5) limited temporal coverage (short capture window, no long-term drift); (6) absence of physical/data-link-layer data (no coverage of jamming or ZigBee/LoRa-specific attacks); (7) no real-time/streaming evaluation; (8) synthetic, fully controlled testbed design limiting ecological realism. Each is a concrete, independently-citable mechanism by which a model (transformer or classical baseline) could learn fixed testbed artifacts (e.g., static device IPs, consistent attack-tool timing/session structure) rather than transferable attack semantics — directly actionable for our RQ3 discussion and limitations section.
- **Stated limitations**: The paper is itself a narrative overview without new empirical measurement of the limitations it lists — it aggregates concerns already raised piecemeal in prior CICIoT2023-based studies rather than independently quantifying them (e.g., no new experiment measuring the encrypted-traffic gap or temporal drift directly).
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Beyond the well-documented class imbalance, CICIoT2023's single-topology, single-session, unencrypted, static-attack-scenario testbed design means any model trained on it risks learning artifacts specific to that simulated smart-home environment rather than attack signal that would transfer to real deployments — a caution directly applicable when interpreting SHAP/Captum attributions as evidence of "real" attack semantics rather than dataset shortcuts.
