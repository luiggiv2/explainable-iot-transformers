- **Models / Methods**: Transformer-based framework treating each network **flow as a token**; flows occurring within the same fixed-duration time window (W=5 s) are grouped together to form one input sequence ("flow-to-window aggregation"), letting self-attention model both intra-flow and inter-flow (concurrent-flow) temporal dependencies. **Architecture fully confirmed from the primary PDF (previously `[VERIFICAR]`)**: this is a **small custom Transformer encoder trained from scratch on tabular per-flow numerical features** (CICFlowMeter statistics) — it is **not** a pretrained language model (not BERT/DistilBERT-based) and does not operate on textualized/tokenized natural-language input. Confirmed architectural hyperparameters (Table 4, fixed across all CV folds after a preliminary grid search over context length only): model dimension **d=64**, number of attention heads **H=4**, feed-forward size **d_ff=128**, number of encoder layers **N=4**, dropout=0.1, context length **L=127** flows per window (T=L+1=128 including a prepended learned [CLS] token) — chosen from a grid search over L∈{1,3,7,15,31,63,127,255,511} as the "elbow" point balancing accuracy gains against inference latency (Fig. 5/Fig. 6). No explicit total-parameter count is reported in the paper, but given d=64/H=4/N=4/d_ff=128 this is a substantially smaller and shallower encoder than DistilBERT (66M params, d=768, N=6, H=12) or even TinyBERT — a useful architectural contrast point for our paper (Route A fine-tunes a pretrained 66M-parameter DistilBERT on textualized flows; this paper trains a small custom Transformer from scratch on tabular per-flow feature vectors grouped into flow-token sequences). Evaluation protocol: "Temporally blocked cross-validation" (10-fold, label-wise stratified folding with adjacency buffers to prevent temporal leakage) — confirmed in detail in Section 4.2/Algorithm 1 of the PDF.
- **Key metrics reported**: **Fully confirmed from the primary PDF, Table 6** (previously resting only on secondary/indexed-snippet corroboration). Across the 10 temporally-ordered CV test blocks (mean ± std): **weighted F1-score = 0.9788 ± 0.0228** (i.e., 97.9% weighted F1, matching the abstract), **macro ROC-AUC = 0.9959 ± 0.0045** (i.e., 99.6% macro ROC-AUC, matching the abstract), and **balanced accuracy = 0.9535 ± 0.0288** (this last metric appears only in the full text, not the abstract; balanced accuracy = mean of per-class recalls, defined in the paper's own glossary as "more robust under class imbalance than raw accuracy"). Per-class recall from the aggregated confusion matrix (Fig. 8): Benign 98.5%, DDoS-ICMP 92.6%, DoS-TCP 93.1%, DDoS-TCP 96.1%, OSScan 96.6%, PortScan 93.5%. Per-class AUC (Fig. 9): Benign/OSScan/PortScan ≈0.998, DDoS-TCP ≈0.995, DoS-TCP ≈0.972, DDoS-ICMP ≈0.964 (the hardest class). Transformer inference latency ≈0.3 ms/window (negligible); worst-case end-to-end pipeline latency (flow extraction + windowing + inference) = 2.33 s for the heaviest DDoS-TCP traffic, still below the 5 s window duration (real-time feasible per the authors' own analysis).
- **IMPORTANT METRIC-TYPE CAVEAT (per explicit task instruction, now doubly confirmed against the primary PDF)**: The headline 97.9% figure is a **weighted F1-score** (harmonic mean of precision/recall weighted by per-class support, a 6-class multi-class metric over a benign + 5-attack subset), and the 99.6% figure is a **macro-averaged ROC-AUC** computed in a One-vs-Rest (OVR) multi-class scheme. **Neither is a macro-F1 score.** These are NOT directly comparable to our own paper's macro-F1 (0.707) without an explicit unit conversion/caveat, because (a) weighted F1 is inflated by the dominant Benign class relative to macro-F1, (b) ROC-AUC and F1 measure different things, and (c) the label spaces differ (this paper: benign + 5 attack classes vs. our broader CICIoT2023 subset). The paper's own balanced accuracy (0.9535) is the closest reported metric to a macro-averaged score, but it is still accuracy-based (mean per-class recall), not F1-based, so no valid direct comparison to our macro-F1 exists even via balanced accuracy. Any related-work comparison in our manuscript MUST spell out these metric-type differences explicitly rather than juxtapose the raw percentages.
- **XAI method (if any)**: None applied in this paper. The full-text confirms only raw self-attention weights as a potential interpretability signal; no dedicated XAI method (SHAP/Captum/LIME/etc.) is used. Confirmed verbatim from the PDF conclusion (Section 7): "further research is needed to improve the interpretability of the attention mechanism, particularly to better understand how attention weights reflect and prioritize relevant inter-flow interactions in IoT traffic" — explicitly flagged as unresolved future work.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Highly relevant to RQ1 as one of the few 2026 transformer papers evaluated specifically on a **CICIoT2023 subset** (our primary dataset), giving a concrete, recent benchmark — but note the metric-type caveat above before any direct numeric comparison. Its flow-to-window design (attention over multiple concurrent flows, small custom encoder trained from scratch on tabular features) is architecturally very different from our single-flow-as-sentence, pretrained-DistilBERT-fine-tuning approach (Route A) — a useful methodological contrast point for the Related Work section. Also directly relevant to RQ2/RQ4: the authors explicitly identify attention-weight interpretability as unresolved future work, which is a gap our Captum/SHAP-based explainability analysis (RQ2) and robustness check (RQ4) address more rigorously and with a dedicated, validated XAI method rather than raw attention weights.
- **Stated limitations**: Confirmed from the PDF's own Section 7 (Conclusion): (1) evaluation covered only a subset of attack scenarios from CICIoT2023 (5 of 33), extending to all 33 scenarios and additional public datasets (ToN-IoT, BoT-IoT) is left as future work; (2) fixed 5-s window/timeout parameters (flow idle timeout 10 s, activity timeout 5 s, window size W=5 s) represent "a practical compromise between near real-time responsiveness and statistical representativeness" and were not subjected to a systematic sensitivity analysis; (3) attention-mechanism interpretability is explicitly flagged as underdeveloped/future work (see XAI method above); (4) the earliest temporal CV block (00–09) showed measurably lower performance (mean F1=0.916 for PortScan, 0.944 for DoS-TCP) attributed to a covariate shift in flow density over time rather than model bias; (5) deployment on real resource-constrained IoT gateway hardware (vs. the RTX 4060 Ti workstation used for evaluation) would require model quantization/pruning, not yet investigated.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Reframing each IoT network flow as a token and grouping concurrent flows into fixed 5-second time-window sequences lets a small, from-scratch Transformer encoder (d=64, 4 layers, 4 heads) capture relationships between simultaneous flows, reaching a 97.9% weighted F1-score and 99.6% macro ROC-AUC on a 6-class (benign + 5-attack) subset of CICIoT2023 under temporally blocked cross-validation, with sub-millisecond per-window inference latency. The authors explicitly flag attention-weight interpretability as unresolved future work — a gap this paper (Route A) is positioned to help fill via Captum/SHAP, applied to a pretrained-encoder fine-tuning approach rather than a from-scratch small Transformer over tabular features.
