- **Dataset(s)**: N-BaIoT (1.8M+ labeled instances, 9 IoT devices, 10 attack classes — Mirai/Gafgyt variants — + benign); CICIoT2023 (subset of 11 attack types selected for computational efficiency)
- **Models / Methods**: Autoencoder-based feature extraction followed by five classifiers — Logistic Regression, Naive Bayes, SVM, XGBoost, MLP; "AutoBoost-IoT" = autoencoder + XGBoost hybrid
- **Key metrics reported**: N-BaIoT overall — XGBoost: acc 99.63%, prec 99.64%, rec 99.63%, F1 99.63%; MLP: acc 91.97%, F1 89.62%; SVM: acc 89.74%, F1 87.22%; Logistic Regression: acc 86.93%, F1 84.45%; Naive Bayes: acc 71.02%, F1 66.87%. N-BaIoT per-class (XGBoost+autoencoder): Mirai_syn/Mirai_udpplain/Gafgyt_scan/Gafgyt_udp 100% F1; Gafgyt_combo lowest at 97.70% F1; Benign 99.88% F1 (100% precision). CICIoT2023 subset: hybrid model 98.94% accuracy, per-class F1 >0.90 across attack categories (exact per-class values not itemized in extracted content — [VERIFICAR]). Ablation: XGBoost alone (no autoencoder) 98.76% accuracy vs. 99.63% with autoencoder on N-BaIoT
- **XAI method (if any)**: None applied in this study; authors explicitly list adding XAI methods as future work
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Useful secondary evidence for RQ1 — shows SVM (89.74% acc, N-BaIoT) trailing XGBoost by ~10 points, consistent with the pattern (linear/kernel methods underperforming tree ensembles on IoT flow features) seen across this cluster, and reports a CICIoT2023 result (98.94% accuracy, subset of 11 attack types) usable as a secondary classical-baseline reference. Also reports compact model-size and per-sample latency figures (XGBoost 1.68 MB / 0.189 ms vs. SVM 21.15 MB / 11.79 ms) directly relevant to RQ5 (accuracy/latency/footprint trade-off), giving a concrete classical-model latency floor to compare our DistilBERT inference time against.
- **Stated limitations**: Not designed to run directly on individual sensors/embedded devices — requires gateway/edge-server-level compute; minor misclassification between closely related attack subtypes sharing packet formats (mirai_udp vs. mirai_udpplain); autoencoder's performance gain over plain XGBoost is modest (98.76% → 99.63% on N-BaIoT), questioning the added architectural complexity; concept drift may degrade performance over time, requiring periodic retraining; authors flag XAI integration and real-world distributed testing as needed future work.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: An autoencoder-plus-XGBoost hybrid reaches near-ceiling per-class F1 on N-BaIoT botnet traffic (100% for several Mirai/Gafgyt subtypes, ≥97.7% for the hardest class) and 98.94% accuracy on a CICIoT2023 subset, while XGBoost alone already achieves 98.76% on N-BaIoT — suggesting the autoencoder's representational benefit is real but modest, and that gains should be weighed against the added inference-time and model-size cost relative to a lean XGBoost baseline (XGBoost: 1.68 MB, 0.189 ms/sample vs. SVM: 21.15 MB, 11.79 ms/sample).
