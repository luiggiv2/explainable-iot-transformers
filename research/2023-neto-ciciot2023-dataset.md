- **Dataset(s)**: CICIoT2023 (this is the original dataset paper — our primary dataset)
- **Models / Methods**: Benchmark evaluation with 5 classical/DL classifiers: Logistic Regression, Perceptron, AdaBoost, Random Forest, Deep Neural Network (DNN); 80/20 train/test split, StandardScaler normalization
- **Attack generation tools (per category, from full text via PMC — relevant to RQ2/RQ3 tool-fingerprint analysis)**: DDoS: hping3, udp-flood, golang-httpflood; Recon: nmap, fping, vulscan; Web-based: DVWA, BeEF, Remot3d; Brute force: nmap + hydra; Spoofing: ettercap; Mirai: adapted Mirai source code. Feature extraction uses 10-packet windows for Recon/Web/Brute-Force/Spoofing and 100-packet windows for DDoS/DoS/Mirai — a per-category processing asymmetry worth checking as a potential dataset artifact (RQ3).
- **Key metrics reported**: Binary (malicious vs. benign): all 5 classifiers >98% accuracy (F1 not explicit for most; Perceptron F1 ≈ 81%). 8-class (attack category) classification: Random Forest and DNN maintain high accuracy with F1 ≈ 70%, while Logistic Regression/Perceptron/AdaBoost degrade significantly. 34-class (individual attack) classification: RF and DNN accuracy similar to 8-class with only ~1% F1 reduction (F1 ≈ 69%); Logistic Regression/Perceptron/AdaBoost drop to <80% accuracy and <50% F1
- **XAI method (if any)**: None (this is the dataset/benchmark paper, no explainability analysis)
- **Relevance to our RQs**: This is the **mandatory foundational citation** for our primary dataset. Defines the dataset's IoT topology (105 devices, 67 IoT + 38 Zigbee/Z-Wave), the standard 34-class/8-class/binary label groupings we will reuse for our own stratified subset (RQ1), and provides the original classical-ML baseline numbers (RF ≈ 70% F1 at 8-class, ≈69% at 34-class) that any of our classical baselines (RF/SVM/XGBoost) should be benchmarked against or expected to exceed. Also documents systematic misclassification patterns (Web-based attacks confused with benign/Recon/spoofing) directly relevant to RQ3 (per-attack-category bias/dataset artifacts).
- **Stated limitations**: Web-based attacks are frequently misclassified as benign, Recon, or spoofing due to similar underlying traffic patterns; the same confusion applies to brute-force attacks; misclassification rates for DDoS, DoS, and Mirai are very low but benign/Recon/spoofing show degraded performance from data-pattern similarity; authors note future work should target individual attack/category-level optimization.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: The dataset's authors show that binary malicious-vs-benign detection is essentially solved (>98% accuracy across all tested classifiers, including simple linear models), but granular classification collapses this advantage — only ensemble/deep models (Random Forest, DNN) sustain moderate F1 (~69-70%) at 8-class and 34-class granularity, while linear models (Logistic Regression, Perceptron) and boosting-lite methods (AdaBoost) fail, and even the best models confuse benign traffic with Reconnaissance, Spoofing, and Web-based attacks due to genuinely overlapping flow-level signatures — establishing the low-F1 baseline that later CICIoT2023 papers (including tree-based/XGBoost pipelines) report substantially improving upon.
