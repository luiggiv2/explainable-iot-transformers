- **Dataset(s)**: UNSW IoT Traces (23 IoT + non-IoT devices, 26 weeks), MonIoTr (46 IoT devices, 34,586 experiments), IoT Sentinel (31 devices, 4 months), YourThings (61 IoT + 5 non-IoT devices, 10 days), IoT-FCSIT (6 IoT + 3 non-IoT devices, in-house, 2 months). NOT CICIoT2023.
- **Models / Methods**: "MIND-IoT" framework: (1) NFStream-based flow feature extraction (85 → 64 features) with GAN-based augmentation; (2) custom "IoT-Tokenize" pipeline — byte-pair encoding, 52,000-token vocabulary plus 64 predefined feature tokens — designed to preserve structural semantics of network flows during tokenization (an alternative to plain-sentence serialization); (3) 6-layer transformer encoder pretrained via masked language modeling on unlabeled IoT traffic; (4) three fine-tuned variants — MIND-MLP, MIND-CNN, MIND-RoBERTa — for binary IoT/non-IoT classification, category recognition, and device identification.
- **Key metrics reported**: Binary classification: MIND-CNN avg F1 = 88.46%; category classification: MIND-CNN avg F1 = 92.21%; device identification: MIND-CNN avg F1 = 88.38%; manufacturer-group classification: 98.14% accuracy, 97.85% F1 (minimal training time).
- **XAI method (if any)**: None; authors reference the transformer's self-attention mechanism qualitatively but apply no attention-visualization, SHAP, or Captum-based explainability.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Relevant to RQ1 as a methodological alternative to our approach: instead of serializing flow features into natural-language sentences (our "proto=TCP srcport=... " template), MIND-IoT designs a custom domain-specific tokenizer/vocabulary for flow features. Useful for the related-work discussion on serialization/tokenization design choices, and its explicitly stated interpretability gap strengthens our XAI-focused contribution (RQ2–RQ4).
- **Stated limitations**: Authors explicitly acknowledge "high computational complexity and limited interpretability present challenges for practical deployment, especially in resource-constrained IoT environments," and recommend future work on model compression, knowledge distillation, or pruning — directly aligned with our RQ5 motivation.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: A custom byte-pair-style tokenizer built specifically to preserve network-flow structure, combined with masked-language-model pretraining, lets a transformer encoder generalize across five independently collected IoT-device datasets and multiple classification granularities, but the authors themselves flag the resulting model's interpretability and compute cost as open problems for constrained IoT deployment.
