- **Dataset(s)**: **Fully confirmed from the primary PDF, Section 4.5 ("Dataset description") — resolves the prior `[VERIFICAR]`.** Three publicly available datasets are used: **ToN-IoT, RT-IoT (RT-IoT2022), and N-BaIoT.** Verbatim: "The experiments are conducted using three publicly available datasets: the ToN-IoT, RT-IoT, and N-BaIoT datasets, which are widely used in the research community for evaluating machine-learning-based intrusion detection systems (IDSs)." ToN-IoT covers realistic simulation of attack patterns including Backdoor, DDoS, DoS, Injection, MITM, Password, Ransomware, Scanning, XSS, plus Normal traffic. RT-IoT includes ARP-Poisoning, DDOS-Slowloris, DOS-SYN-Hping, MQTT-Publish, Metasploit-Brute-Force, and various NMAP-based attacks (FIN-SCAN, OS-DETECT, TCP-SCAN, UDP-SCAN, XMAS-TREE), plus benign Thing-Speak/Wipro-Bulb traffic. N-BaIoT includes Mirai and Gafgyt botnet traffic (Mirai_ACK/Scan/SYN/UDP/UDPPlain, Gafgyt_Combo/Junk/Scan/TCP/UDP) plus Benign.
- **Models / Methods**: "DistillGuard" — a Transformer-based **teacher** model using a hybrid attention mechanism (multi-head self-attention (MHSA) + cross-attention layers over temporal and network features) distilled via **Selective Gradient-Based Knowledge Distillation (SG-KD)** into a lightweight fully-connected **student** model (3 dense layers, ReLU activations) for resource-constrained IoT deployment. Confirmed from PDF Section 4.2 ("Model architecture"): the Teacher has **6 transformer layers, 8 attention heads in the MHSA mechanism, hidden dimension 512 per layer, feed-forward layers of 2048 neurons**, cross-attention dimensionality d_c=512, trained for 50 epochs, batch size 32, learning rate 0.001, Adam optimizer, dropout 0.1. The Student is trained via SG-KD with distillation temperature T=2.0, learning rate 0.0005 (Adam), batch size 64, 50 epochs.
- **Key metrics reported**: **Fully confirmed from the primary PDF, Tables 5–8 (previously `[VERIFICAR]` — the abstract alone is vague).** Representative headline results (Tables 7–8, "Proposed Model" rows, comparing Teacher vs. Student against 5 other state-of-the-art IDS techniques):
  - **Binary attack detection** (Table 7): ToN-IoT — Teacher Acc 96.02%/F1 96.01%, Student Acc 96.43%/F1 96.40%; RT-IoT — Teacher Acc 98.63%/F1 98.71%, Student Acc 98.81%/F1 98.82%; N-BaIoT — Teacher Acc 87.14%/F1 87.45%, Student Acc 87.13%/F1 87.43%.
  - **Multi-class attack detection** (Table 8): ToN-IoT — Teacher Acc 94.98%/F1 95.08%, Student Acc 88.13%/F1 86.57%; RT-IoT — Teacher Acc 99.36%/F1 93.11%, Student Acc 99.15%/F1 93.17%; N-BaIoT — Teacher and Student both in the ~87–99% F1/accuracy range depending on class (see PDF Table 8 for exact per-dataset decimal values).
  - Crucially for **RQ5** (accuracy/latency/footprint trade-off): the Student model achieves comparable-to-superior accuracy with **far fewer parameters** than the Teacher — e.g., multi-class: ToN-IoT Teacher 630,000 params (35.00 MB) vs. Student 13,000 params (2.80 MB); RT-IoT Teacher 502,500 params (38.00 MB) vs. Student 16,000 params (2.00 MB); N-BaIoT Teacher 3,745,803 params (75.43 MB) vs. Student 16,523 params (2.50 MB) — a >200× parameter reduction in some cases (Tables 5, 9, 10). DistillGuard also outperforms four other KD baselines (AKD, Collaborative KD, GAN-based KD, LKD-STNN) in accuracy while using up to 5× fewer parameters (Table 11).
  - Overall abstract-level claim ("DistillGuard achieves superior detection accuracy and efficiency compared to existing state-of-the-art IDS models") is thus substantiated with precise per-dataset, per-task numbers in the full text.
- **XAI method (if any)**: Gradient-based analysis — "Gradient Contribution Heatmaps," "layer-wise contribution analysis," and "gradient selection impact analysis" (Section 4.7) used to identify which transformer layers and attention heads drive detections. Confirmed finding: layers 3 and 4 (mid-level) consistently contribute most to knowledge transfer/detection across all three datasets (Fig. 11, Fig. 12), while early layers (1–2) and late layers (5–6) contribute less — interpreted as mid-level representations capturing the most relevant intrusion-detection features. This is an attention/gradient-based interpretability approach, not SHAP/LIME/Captum.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Directly relevant to **RQ5** — parallels our accuracy/latency/footprint trade-off question using knowledge distillation to compress a Transformer-IDS teacher into a lightweight student for IoT edge deployment, now with concrete, confirmed parameter-count and accuracy figures for comparison. Also a precedent within Cluster 3 for gradient/attention-based (rather than SHAP/Captum-based) interpretability of a Transformer IDS, and for our target-journal fit (Computers & Security is one of our candidate venues).
- **Stated limitations**: Not explicitly listed as a dedicated "Limitations" subsection in the PDF (the paper moves from Discussion directly to Conclusion), but the Discussion (Section 5) and Section 7's own future-work framing implicitly note: (1) adversarial robustness is evaluated only via FGSM on three specific datasets — generalization to other datasets/attack types not tested; (2) the paper acknowledges deployment on real hardware accelerators (Edge TPUs, FPGAs, NVIDIA Jetson) as still requiring further optimization ("Real-Time Performance & Low Latency" and "Scalability in Large IoT Deployments" flagged as open challenges in the Discussion).
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: A Transformer teacher's hybrid self- and cross-attention weights (6 layers, 8 heads, d=512) can be distilled via Selective Gradient-Based Knowledge Distillation into a lightweight fully-connected student network (as few as 1,410–16,523 parameters, up to ~250× smaller) for IoT intrusion detection on ToN-IoT, RT-IoT, and N-BaIoT, achieving comparable or better accuracy than the teacher while preserving gradient-based, layer-wise explainability (mid-layers 3–4 identified as most informative) — demonstrating that attention-based interpretability need not be sacrificed for the model compression required by edge deployment.
