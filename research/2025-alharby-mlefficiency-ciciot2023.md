- **Relevance to our RQs**: Directly relevant to RQ1 and RQ5 — reports a highly efficient Decision-Tree-based classical baseline on CICIoT2023 (a 4-class DoS/DDoS/Mirai/Benign subset) achieving near-ceiling accuracy/F1 with drastically reduced training/inference time, giving us a compute-efficiency reference point to contrast against our transformer's latency and footprint. - **Stated limitations**: Undersampling for class balance risks losing information from majority classes, potentially reducing the model's ability to capture full attack-pattern diversity; notable confusion between DoS and DDoS due to similar traffic characteristics; real-world deployment requires addressing scalability, security, and privacy beyond lab validation; adversarial-attack vulnerability is a concern, though decision trees are comparatively more interpretable than black-box alternatives; maintaining 99.99% accuracy under privacy-preserving methods (e.g., differential privacy noise) is noted as challenging.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Restricting CICIoT2023 to a class-balanced 4-category subset (Benign/DoS/DDoS/Mirai) and applying Random-Forest-Regressor-based feature selection lets a simple Decision Tree reach near-perfect accuracy/F1 (99.99%) while cutting training time by over 98% and prediction time by over 99% relative to prior CICIoT2023 benchmarks — but the persistent DoS/DDoS confusion the author reports suggests this near-ceiling performance partly reflects the coarser 4-class grouping rather than fully resolved discrimination at the finer attack-subtype level.
