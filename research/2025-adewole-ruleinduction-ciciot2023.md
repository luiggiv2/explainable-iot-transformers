- **Dataset(s)**: CIC-IDS2017 (2,830,743 samples, 79 features, 15 class labels); CICIoT2023 (45,019,234 samples, 40 features, 33 attack types + benign)
- **Models / Methods**: Random Forest, AdaBoost, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost (gradient-boosted tree family compared against RF/AdaBoost); rule-induction algorithm extracts if-then decision rules from the trained tree ensembles for model explanation
- **Key metrics reported**: CIC-IDS2017 binary (XGBoost): accuracy 99.91%, precision 99.91%, recall 99.91%, F1 99.91%, AUC-ROC 99.88%, MCC 99.69%. CIC-IDS2017 multi-class (XGBoost): accuracy 99.88%, precision 99.87%, recall 99.88%, F1 99.87%, AUC-ROC 99.99%, MCC 99.61%. CICIoT2023 binary (XGBoost): accuracy 98.54%, precision 98.56%, recall 98.54%, F1 98.55%, AUC-ROC 93.06%, MCC 84.83%
- **XAI method (if any)**: Rule-induction (if-then rule extraction from tree ensembles) rather than Captum/SHAP — 39 rules generated for CIC-IDS2017 binary classification, 41 rules for multi-class classification; a symbolic/surrogate alternative to feature-attribution XAI
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Provides a directly citable, verifiable **XGBoost baseline on our exact primary dataset (CICIoT2023)**: 98.54% accuracy / 98.55% F1 / 93.06% AUC-ROC for binary classification — a strong reference point for RQ1 (competitive classical-baseline performance) and a useful sanity check for our own XGBoost tabular baseline. The rule-induction explainability approach also offers a contrasting, non-gradient-based interpretability method to juxtapose against our Captum/SHAP results for RQ2 (do symbolic rules and gradient attributions agree on influential features?).
- **Stated limitations**: Scalability of the approach to large IoT networks and massive real deployments remains untested; performance metrics such as inference time may vary across heterogeneous resource-constrained IoT devices; CICIoT2023 experiments reported are binary-only (malicious vs. benign) in the extracted results, not the finer 8-class/34-class taxonomy.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: On the CICIoT2023 dataset, an XGBoost classifier achieves 98.5%+ accuracy and F1 for binary malicious/benign detection but a comparatively modest 93.06% AUC-ROC, and the authors show that a compact set of ~40 extracted if-then rules can reproduce the tree ensemble's decision logic in human-readable form — indicating that rule-induction is a viable, lower-cost alternative explanation method to feature-attribution techniques for IoT tabular flow data.
