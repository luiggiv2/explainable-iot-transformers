# Exploratory Data Analysis of a Network Telescope Traffic and Prediction of Port Probing Rates
- **Authors**: Mehdi Zakroum, Abdellah Houmz, Mounir Ghogho, Ghita Mezzour, Abdelkader Lahmadi, Jérôme François, Mohammed El Koutbi
- **Year / Venue**: 2018, IEEE International Conference on Intelligence and Security Informatics (ISI 2018), Miami, FL. [VERIFICAR quartile — IEEE conference, not journal-ranked; preprint also on arXiv:1812.09790]
- **DOI**: 10.1109/ISI.2018.8587323
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.09790
- **Cluster**: 4
- **Dataset(s)**: A /20 network telescope (darknet) capture, November 2014–October 2017 (~2 TB), comprising ~4.5 billion TCP SYN packets from over 64 million distinct source IPs.
- **Models / Methods**: Exploratory/statistical traffic analysis (destination-port aggregation, "top prober" identification via a probing-rate threshold of >150 TCP SYN/day, geolocation breakdown) followed by autoregressive (VAR) and LSTM-based time-series forecasting of per-port probing rates.
- **Key metrics reported**: Destination **port 23 (Telnet) alone accounts for 50.34%** of all telescope TCP-SYN traffic. Next most-targeted: port 22/SSH (5.66%), port 2323/alt-Telnet (2.81%), port 445/SMB (2.35%), port 3389/RDP (1.94%), port 80/HTTP (1.89%), port 7547/TR-069-CWMP (1.85%), port 1433/MSSQL (1.83%), port 5358 (1.57%), port 8080 (1.23%). Only 35 of 65,535 possible ports generate 80% of all scanning traffic, and 550 ports generate 90%. Critically, "top network probers" (>150 SYN/day, ~1,500 sources) disproportionately target **port 22 (SSH) over port 23 (Telnet)** — the opposite skew from the aggregate population, which is dominated by high-volume, low-persistence (largely Mirai-like) scanners favoring Telnet.
- **XAI method (if any)**: None.
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Independent, large-scale (3-year, multi-billion-packet) empirical ground truth for RQ2's Reconnaissance/scanning category: destination-port concentration (23, 22, 2323, 445, 3389) and TCP-SYN-flag dominance are the expected discriminative signal. The Telnet-vs-SSH split by scanner "tier" is a useful nuance for RQ3 — it shows destination-port importance for "recon" traffic is not monolithic but depends on the scanning population, relevant when auditing whether the model's port-based attributions match a specific scanner archetype or a blended average.
- **Stated limitations**: A network telescope only observes unsolicited inbound traffic to unused IP space, so it cannot capture reconnaissance against live, routed hosts, nor any application-layer follow-up after a successful scan; the "top prober" definition explicitly excludes distributed/coordinated scanning campaigns, so results characterize single-source scanners more than botnet-coordinated reconnaissance; the capture window (2014–2017) predates much of the recent IoT protocol landscape, so port popularity may have shifted.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Scanning traffic on the open Internet is extremely port-concentrated — Telnet alone draws over half of all probes in a three-year darknet capture — but this concentration is driven by low-effort, high-volume bots, while the small subset of the highest-throughput individual scanners instead prioritizes SSH, showing that scanner identity systematically changes scan-target distribution.
