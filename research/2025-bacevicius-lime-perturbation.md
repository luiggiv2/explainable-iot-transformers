- **Dataset(s)**: **Confirmed from the primary PDF, Section 3 ("Dataset") — resolves the prior `[VERIFICAR]`.** The paper uses **both CIC-IDS2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS-2018**, explicitly **merged into a single combined working dataset** to address CSE-CIC-IDS-2018's severe class imbalance (82.66% of records in the dominant class) and increase representation of underrepresented classes. Verbatim: "merging the two datasets could increase the representation of underrepresented classes, enhancing the training of machine learning models... Finally, our dataset contained 28 classes. After feature filtering, 32 features were retained as follows: 7 categorical binary features (frequency skewed) and 25 numerical features." Class list (Fig. 2 caption) includes: BENIGN, DDoS, PortScan, Bot, Infiltration, Web Attack Brute Force, Web Attack XSS, FTP-Patator, SSH-Patator, DoS Slowloris, DoS Slowhttptest, DoS Hulk, DoS GoldenEye, Heartbleed, DoS Attacks-SlowHTTPTest, Brute Force-Web, Brute Force-XSS, SQL Injection, DDoS Attacks-LOIC-HTTP, Infiltration, DoS Attacks-GoldenEye, DoS Attacks-Slowloris, FTP-BruteForce, SSH-Bruteforce, DDoS Attack-LOIC-UDP, DDoS Attack-HOIC (28 classes total, spanning both source datasets' labels). Note: the paper's own Conclusion (Section 7) refers to this merged dataset loosely as "datasets such as CIC-IDS-2018" — but the actual experimental dataset (Section 3/Table in the PDF) is the CIC-IDS2017 + CSE-CIC-IDS-2018 merge, not CIC-IDS-2018 alone.
- **Models / Methods**: **Confirmed full model list from the primary PDF, Table 2 — resolves the prior `[VERIFICAR]`.** Five classifiers with varying complexity: **Decision Tree (DT), Random Forest (RF), k-Nearest Neighbors (K-NN), XGBoost (XGB), and CatBoost (CATB)**. LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) is applied to each, using ridge regression as the surrogate model, 5,000 perturbed samples per explanation, top-10 features evaluated, 30 independent explanation runs per configuration. Modified perturbation-sampling strategies tested: Gaussian (baseline), Beta (α=0.5, β=0.5), Gamma (k=1, θ=1), Pareto (shape α∈{3,4,5,6,7}, scale c=1), and Weibull (shape k∈{0.1,0.4,0.5,0.7,0.8}, scale c=1) — 13 distribution configurations total, evaluated against 3 test-instance labels (most common class, second-most-common class, least-common class).
- **Key metrics reported**: Confirmed from the primary PDF, Tables 3–6. Classifier training performance (Table 3, weighted/macro accuracy-precision-recall-F1): XGBoost achieved the highest weighted accuracy (0.99954) and macro-F1 (0.86501); Random Forest close behind (weighted accuracy 0.99875, macro-F1 0.86095); DT, K-NN, CatBoost also >0.97 weighted accuracy. Explanation **fidelity** (R², Table 4): Pareto (shape=3) achieves the best fidelity for Decision Tree (**R²=0.9267**) and K-NN (**R²=0.9971**); Weibull (shape=0.1) achieves the best fidelity for XGBoost (R²=0.6042) and CatBoost (R²=0.5511); Random Forest's best is also Pareto (shape=7, R²=0.4811). All tested non-Gaussian distributions substantially outperform the Gaussian baseline (whose fidelity is as low as R²=0.1579 averaged, Table 6). Explanation **stability** (Jaccard similarity S, Table 5): Pareto distributions consistently yield the highest stability, particularly for **K-NN (S up to 0.9907–0.9910)** and **Decision Tree (S up to 0.9797–0.9788)**; Gaussian stability is markedly lower (as low as S=0.7099 for DT). Averaged across models (Table 6), Pareto (shape=3) has the best combined fidelity+stability rank, closely followed by Pareto (shape=7); Gaussian ranks worst on both metrics. The abstract's "up to 78% improvement in explanation fidelity" and "K-NN/DT show highest stability and reliability" claims are thus fully substantiated by the primary-text tables.
- **XAI method (if any)**: LIME, with modified perturbation-sampling strategies using Weibull, Gamma, Beta, and Pareto distributions compared against the standard Gaussian perturbation scheme — evaluated via two custom metrics: explanation fidelity (R², how well the LIME ridge-regression surrogate approximates the underlying classifier's local decision boundary) and explanation stability (S, mean pairwise Jaccard similarity of top-10 feature sets across 30 independent explanation runs).
- **Relevance to our RQs**: Directly relevant to **RQ4** — provides a concrete, now fully confirmed precedent for systematically varying the perturbation mechanism used to generate/evaluate explanations in an IDS context and measuring resulting changes in explanation fidelity/stability via a Jaccard-similarity-based stability metric, methodologically parallel (though not identical) to our planned Spearman-rank-correlation-based robustness protocol. Differs from our design in that the perturbation targets LIME's internal sampling distribution rather than the input instance itself, and in the choice of stability statistic (Jaccard set-overlap vs. our planned rank correlation) — worth noting explicitly as a methodological contrast in the Related Work / Methodology sections.
- **Stated limitations**: Confirmed from the primary PDF, Section 6 (Discussion) and Section 7 (Conclusion) — no dedicated "Limitations" subsection, but the Discussion explicitly frames open issues as future work: (1) the study applies a single perturbation distribution uniformly across all features per configuration, rather than tailoring the disturbance method per individual attribute's own distributional/structural characteristics (explicitly proposed as future work); (2) perturbation methods for categorical and binary attributes are not yet specialized (frequency-weighted sampling, class-conditional perturbation, and flipping strategies for binary features are proposed as future improvements rather than implemented in this study); (3) fidelity and stability gains from the modified distributions are much smaller for complex, non-linear models (XGBoost, Random Forest, CatBoost) than for simpler models (DT, K-NN) — the paper explicitly states "challenges remain in achieving similarly high levels of fidelity and stability for more complex models like XGB and Random Forests, which exhibit intricate, non-linear decision boundaries"; (4) evaluation is LIME-specific (no SHAP or Captum/Integrated-Gradients comparison) and restricted to tabular network-flow features from the CIC-IDS dataset family.
- **Quotable finding (paraphrased)**: Replacing LIME's default Gaussian perturbation sampling with heavy-tailed distributions — particularly Pareto (shape=3) — substantially improves both explanation fidelity (R² up to 0.93–0.997) and stability (Jaccard similarity up to ~0.98–0.99) for intrusion-detection classifiers evaluated on a merged CIC-IDS2017/CSE-CIC-IDS-2018 dataset (28 classes), with the largest gains for simpler models (Decision Tree, K-Nearest Neighbors); gains are markedly smaller for more complex models (XGBoost, Random Forest, CatBoost) whose non-linear decision boundaries remain harder for any LIME variant to approximate faithfully.
